# Personal Video Coach

## Overview

Professional video script creation for any context where a personal video presentation is needed — job applications, elevator pitches, company introductions, crowdfunding pitches, conference proposals, or social media content. Helps structure your message, write compelling scripts optimized for spoken delivery, and prepare for recording.

## When to Use

- Creating a video introduction for a job application or networking opportunity
- Writing an elevator pitch video (60-90 seconds) to showcase your value
- Preparing a video cover letter as part of an application
- Scripting a crowdfunding or startup pitch video
- Creating personal brand videos for LinkedIn, YouTube, or other platforms
- Preparing a conference talk proposal video or speaker introduction
- Recording a video bio for a company website or team page
- Crafting video scripts for any context where you need to present yourself on camera

## How It Works

Describe your goal, audience, and key points you want to convey. The skill structures a script optimized for spoken delivery — conversational tone, natural pacing, and strong openings/closings. It provides guidance on delivery, timing, and visual cues to help you record confidently.

## Steps

### 1. Define the Video Goal

- Clarify the purpose: job pitch, introduction, fundraising, thought leadership
- Identify your audience and what they care about
- Determine the ideal length (30 sec / 60 sec / 90 sec / 2-3 min)
- Choose the platform (LinkedIn, email attachment, YouTube, Loom, etc.)

### 2. Structure the Script

- **Opening hook (first 5 seconds):** Grab attention — a surprising fact, a question, a bold statement, or a relatable problem
- **Who you are (10-15 seconds):** Your name, role, and one-line value proposition
- **The problem/opportunity (15-20 seconds):** What challenge you solve or what you bring to the table
- **Your unique angle (20-30 seconds):** Specific experience, skills, or perspective that sets you apart
- **Proof points (15-20 seconds):** One or two concrete achievements with metrics
- **Call to action (10-15 seconds):** What you want the viewer to do — visit a link, schedule a call, reply to the email

### 3. Write for Spoken Delivery

- Use short sentences (15-20 words max per sentence)
- Write in a conversational tone — read it aloud to test the flow
- Avoid jargon and corporate-speak; sound like a human
- Include natural pauses and emphasis markers
- Time the script: ~150 words per minute for natural delivery

### 4. Add Delivery Notes

- Mark where to smile, pause, or change vocal energy
- Note visual cues: "hold up the product," "point to screen," "gesture to emphasize"
- Identify B-roll opportunities: screen recordings, photos, text overlays

### 5. Prepare for Recording

- **Setup:** Good lighting (natural window light is free and effective), quiet room, stable camera at eye level
- **Framing:** Head and shoulders, centered, looking into the lens (not the screen)
- **Energy:** Stand if possible, smile, speak 10-15% more energetically than normal conversation
- **Practice:** Do 3-5 run-throughs. The first take is rarely the best.

### 6. Video Script Templates

**Job Pitch (60 sec):**
```
[HOOK: 5 sec] Did you know [interesting stat about the industry]?
[INTRO: 10 sec] I'm [Name], a [role] who [one-line value prop].
[PROBLEM: 15 sec] Companies in [industry] struggle with [challenge].
[SOLUTION: 20 sec] At [past company], I [achievement with metric].
[CALL TO ACTION: 10 sec] I'd love to bring this to [target company].
  Let's talk — [contact info].
```

**Elevator Pitch (30 sec):**
```
[HOOK] You know how [common frustration]?
[INTRO] I'm [Name], and I help [audience] [key outcome].
[PROOF] For example, I [one achievement with metric].
[CTA] If you're looking to [goal], let's connect.
```

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Reading like a script** — If it sounds like you're reading, rewrite it to be more conversational. Use contractions and natural phrasing.
2. **Starting too slow** — The first 5 seconds determine if someone keeps watching. Lead with your strongest hook.
3. **Too long** — Shorter is almost always better. A tight 60-second video outperforms a rambling 3-minute one.
4. **Looking at yourself, not the lens** — Practice looking into the camera lens, not at your own face on screen. Tape a photo of a friend next to the lens if it helps.
5. **No call to action** — Every video should end with a clear next step for the viewer.
