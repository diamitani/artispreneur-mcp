# Sales Commander

## Overview
Acts as a strategic sales ops commander — helping design, audit, and optimize end-to-end sales strategy. Covers team structure, territory planning, quota setting, process design, tech stack, and performance management.

## When to Use
- Building a sales strategy from scratch (startup, new product line, market entry)
- Auditing and optimizing an existing sales org for efficiency or scale
- Designing compensation plans, quota models, or territory assignments
- Evaluating and selecting sales tech stack (CRM, outreach, analytics)
- Restructuring teams: SDR-to-AE ratios, inbound vs outbound split, vertical specialization
- Creating sales playbooks, battle cards, and competitive positioning

## How It Works
1. Assess current state: team size, revenue, funnel metrics, bottlenecks.
2. Define target state: revenue goals, market segment, ideal customer profile (ICP).
3. Design the operating model: roles, processes, tools, metrics.
4. Build an implementation roadmap with milestones and owners.

## Steps
1. **Discovery** — Ask about current team structure, revenue targets, sales cycle length, win rates, and pain points.
2. **Diagnose** — Identify the top 1-3 constraints limiting growth (e.g., pipeline shortage, low conversion, churn).
3. **Design** — Propose structural changes: team roles, territory splits, compensation adjustments, process improvements.
4. **Tooling** — Recommend or evaluate CRM, sequencing tools, analytics, and enablement platforms.
5. **Metrics & cadence** — Define KPIs (activity, pipeline, conversion, revenue), meeting rhythms, and dashboards.
6. **Playbook** — Draft sales playbook sections: ICP definition, qualification framework (MEDDIC, BANT, etc.), objection handling, demo flows.
7. **Roadmap** — Lay out 30/60/90-day implementation plan with clear owners.

## Common Pitfalls
- **Over-engineering**: A 5-person startup doesn't need enterprise SDR/AE ratios. Match complexity to stage.
- **Ignoring rep input**: Top-down strategy that ignores frontline feedback breeds resentment and poor adoption.
- **Vanity metrics**: Tracking calls made without pipeline created rewards activity over outcomes.
- **No change management**: New processes fail without training, documentation, and reinforcement.
- **Skipping ICP definition**: Without a clear ICP, everything downstream (messaging, targeting, qualification) drifts.

## Inputs

- `current_state` -- team size, revenue, funnel metrics, sales cycle length, win rates, pain points
- `target_state` -- revenue goals, market segment, ideal customer profile (ICP)

## Outputs

- An operating-model design: roles and SDR-to-AE ratios, territory splits, compensation/quota models, tech-stack recommendations (CRM, outreach, analytics), KPI definitions with meeting rhythms and dashboards, playbook sections (ICP definition, qualification framework like MEDDIC/BANT, objection handling, demo flows), and a 30/60/90-day implementation roadmap with clear owners

## Example

A startup entering a new market: discovery (8 reps, $2M ARR, 90-day cycle, 18% win rate) -> diagnose pipeline shortage as constraint #1 -> design a 3-SDR/2-AE pod with vertical territories -> recommend HubSpot + Outreach -> define activity/pipeline/conversion KPIs with a weekly cadence -> draft the playbook -> lay out the 90-day plan with owners per milestone.

## Errors & edge cases

- **Over-engineering** -- a 5-person startup doesn't need enterprise SDR/AE ratios; match complexity to stage.
- **Ignoring rep input** -- top-down strategy without frontline feedback breeds resentment and poor adoption.
- **Vanity metrics** -- tracking calls made without pipeline created rewards activity over outcomes.
- **No change management** -- new processes fail without training, documentation, and reinforcement.
- **Skipped ICP definition** -- without a clear ICP, messaging, targeting, and qualification all drift.
